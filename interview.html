SPEAK ABOUT YOUR SKILLS WITH CLARITY

Prepare to explain, not memorise.

Use these questions to practise clear thinking. The sample frameworks are starting points—adapt them to your actual learning and experience.

Interview question bank

Open a question, think of your own response first, then compare it with the suggested answer structure. For scenario questions, explain your reasoning and what you would verify.

1. What is digital marketing?

Explain that it is the use of digital channels and tools to understand audiences, create and communicate value, support a customer journey and measure outcomes. Mention that it is broader than posting or advertising.

2. How are marketing, advertising and sales different?

Marketing is the wider process of understanding needs and creating value. Advertising is paid promotion. Sales helps a potential customer make a purchase decision and complete a transaction. They support one another.

3. What are the 4 Ps?

Product, Price, Place and Promotion. Explain each with a practical example and show how the decisions need to fit one customer promise.

4. What is a target audience?

A group of people a business aims to understand and serve. A useful audience description includes a relevant need, context or behaviour—not just a broad age range.

5. What is a customer persona?

A research-informed summary of a recurring audience pattern. Explain that it should be based on evidence and should not become a fictional stereotype.

6. What is a conversion?

A defined action that matters to an objective, such as a valid enquiry, booking or purchase. Always explain what counts as the event.

7. What is SEO, and can you guarantee rankings?

SEO improves a website so search engines can understand it and people can find useful pages. Rankings cannot be guaranteed. Describe the work you can control: technical health, helpful content, clear page structure and measurement.

8. What is search intent?

The need or goal behind a search. A person may want to learn, find a particular site, compare options or complete an action. A useful page matches that need.

9. What makes a landing page effective?

Clear audience and offer, relevant headline, useful details, evidence, readable layout, one clear CTA and a working mobile journey. Mention task-based testing.

10. How would you choose a social platform for a small business?

Check whether the audience is there, whether the content format fits, whether the team can maintain it and whether enquiries can be handled. Do not recommend every platform automatically.

11. What is a content pillar?

A repeatable theme used to organise content around audience needs, such as product education, FAQs, behind-the-scenes proof or customer stories.

12. What is the difference between reach and impressions?

Reach generally describes unique accounts or people shown the content; impressions count displays and may include repeat displays. Exact definitions can vary by platform.

13. What is CTR?

Click-through rate is clicks divided by impressions, multiplied by 100. State which clicks and impressions are used because platforms may define them differently.

14. What is CPC?

Cost per click is spend divided by the defined number of clicks. It is an efficiency measure, not proof that the traffic is valuable.

15. What is ROAS, and is it profit?

Return on ad spend is attributed revenue divided by advertising spend. It is not profit because product costs, fulfilment, salaries, overheads, taxes and other expenses are not subtracted.

16. Why is email permission important?

People should understand and agree to the kind of marketing messages they will receive. Explain clear signup wording, permission records, an easy unsubscribe route and list hygiene.

17. What is email segmentation?

Grouping subscribers using relevant, appropriately collected information so messages are more useful. Do not collect sensitive or unnecessary data just to create more segments.

18. What is affiliate marketing?

A commercial arrangement where a publisher refers an audience to a merchant and may earn commission after a defined action. Suitability and transparency should matter more than commission size.

19. How should an influencer or affiliate disclose a material relationship?

Use a clear, prominent disclosure close to the recommendation and follow the applicable rules and platform requirements. Do not hide it in an unrelated footer or use ambiguous wording.

20. How would you report campaign performance?

Restate the objective, define each metric and time window, show what changed, separate observations from interpretation and recommend one evidence-based next step. Mention data limitations.

21. A campaign gets clicks but no enquiries. What would you check?

Validate tracking, audience relevance, ad-to-page promise, mobile usability, form errors, price clarity and lead quality before increasing spend.

22. What would you do if a client asks for guaranteed results?

Clarify what can and cannot be controlled. Explain realistic deliverables, assumptions, risks and measures. Never promise guaranteed rankings, leads, revenue or virality.

23. What belongs in a freelance proposal?

Client problem, approach, deliverables, timeline, responsibilities, price, payment milestones, revision limits, exclusions and approval process.

24. How can a beginner build a portfolio without client work?

Create clearly labelled practice projects using fictional or permissioned scenarios. Show the brief, reasoning, sample deliverables, assumptions and reflection. Do not invent client names, results or testimonials.

25. Tell me about a project you have completed.

Use a simple structure: Situation, Task, Action, Result or learning. For a practice project, say it was fictional. Explain what you personally did, how you made decisions and what you would improve.

Practice tip: record a 60–90 second answer on your phone. Listen for jargon, unsupported claims and missing examples. Then try again in simpler language.
