STRATEGY5 MIN READ

A funnel is a thinking model, not a promise.

A funnel helps a team consider what someone may need before, during and after discovering an offer. Real customer journeys can loop, pause or stop.

Open the example and activity

Maple Lane example: a new neighbour discovers the breakfast box, checks the menu and collection time, then decides whether it fits their morning. Another person may ask a friend first or return days later.

Your activity: write one helpful message for discovery, one for comparison and one for someone ready to order. Do not assume every person follows the same sequence.

METRICS4 MIN READ

A percentage is meaningless without its denominator.

Before reporting a conversion rate, define the action, the group being counted and the date range.

Open the worked calculation

Suppose a fictional bakery receives 60 valid enquiries and 18 become orders during the same reporting period.

18 ÷ 60 × 100 = 30%

This is an enquiry-to-order rate. It is not orders divided by website visits. Keep the stage names clear.

CONTENT5 MIN READ

Build a content calendar from real questions.

Start with audience questions, not a list of formats. Useful themes might include product details, how-to help, behind-the-scenes process and customer support.

Open the planning exercise

Write three questions a local bakery customer might ask. Turn each into a post idea and add one useful next step. Balance helpful posts with promotional messages.

  • Question: What is inside the breakfast box?
  • Post idea: a clear ingredient and portion guide.
  • Next step: view the current menu and check availability.

SEO6 MIN READ

Search intent comes before keyword placement.

A keyword is a clue about a searcher's question. A useful page answers the actual need rather than repeating a phrase unnaturally.

Open the example

Someone searching “breakfast boxes near me” likely needs local availability, price, collection or delivery details. A long generic article about breakfast may not solve that task.

Your activity: choose one search phrase, describe the likely task, then list the information the page must provide.

WEBSITES & CONVERSION5 MIN READ

More traffic cannot fix a confusing page.

Before increasing campaign spend, check whether visitors can understand the offer, price, availability and next action on a phone.

Open the five-point page check
  1. Can a visitor explain the offer after a quick scan?
  2. Are price and availability easy to find?
  3. Is the main action specific?
  4. Does the form ask only for necessary information?
  5. Does the page work at mobile width?
Learn page planning in Module 02 →
EMAIL4 MIN READ

A useful email list starts with permission.

Do not add people to marketing emails just because they asked a question or made a purchase. Explain what they are signing up for and make it easy to unsubscribe.

Open the beginner checklist
  • Tell people what kind of messages they will receive.
  • Collect consent through a clear action.
  • Keep records and respect preferences.
  • Include a working unsubscribe route.
  • Review applicable laws and platform rules before sending.

CAMPAIGN NAMING TOOLTOOL 02

Build a tagged campaign link

UTM parameters help distinguish campaign traffic in analytics reports. Use consistent lowercase names and never place personal information in a URL.

YOUR TAGGED LINKhttps://example.com/menu?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=breakfast_box_launch&utm_content=reel_1

Use the sample or enter your own destination URL.

Learning note: CTR, conversion rate and ROAS definitions can vary by platform, attribution model and reporting setup. Always state the exact action, denominator, attribution method and date range in real reports.
